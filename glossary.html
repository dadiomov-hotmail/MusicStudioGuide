Words

AspectOne way of describing every moment: harmony, texture, dynamics, loudness, timbre or tempo.
Base sequenceAn aspect's values, ten per second, before smoothing ("raw").
BoundaryA moment where something changes: a peak of novelty.
ChromaTwelve numbers: how much of each pitch class is sounding, all octaves together.
CoverageHow much of the piece a section's occurrences fill, beyond the section itself.
FeaturesThe smoothed, normalised values the structure views compare.
FitnessScore and coverage combined (harmonic mean). High when a section repeats closely and fills much of the piece.
FlatAn aspect that does not change at all in this piece.
KernelThe checkerboard that measures novelty; L is its half-width.
MFCCNumbers that describe the spectral shape (the colour) of a sound. The Studio uses twelve and leaves out the one for loudness.
NoveltyHow different the music after a moment is from the music before it.
OccurrenceA place where the selected segment is found, itself included.
Path, path familyThe cyan lines: the alignments of a segment with all its occurrences.
Persistence (k/m)At how many of the scales that can see a moment there is a peak close by.
ReturnAn occurrence with at least 10 seconds of other music before it. An immediate repeat is not a return.
Scale (L)How many seconds the novelty kernel looks back and ahead.
Scape plotThe triangle of all sections, coloured by fitness.
SSMSelf-similarity matrix.
TempogramHow strongly the onsets pulse at each tempo, over time. Cyclic: doublings folded together.
Threshold, penaltyOnly the strongest cells of the matrix are kept; the rest get a negative value, so that the search avoids them.
ThumbnailThe section with the highest fitness.
