How the views can mislead

Ten habits that keep you out of trouble.

  1. Check the aspect. M, T, N and R all show the aspect in the box. "No return" in harmony may be a clear return in texture.
  2. Each row, its own scale. In the stack, and per row in novelty, heights are relative to that row. Never compare across rows.
  3. The edges are blind. Nothing can be said within L of the start or the end, and the jump where the curve begins is not a boundary.
  4. Look at persistence, not only height. A tall peak seen at two scales is a detail.
  5. Raise the minimum length. A thumbnail that does not survive 20 or 30 seconds is a motif or a loop.
  6. Fitness is not quality. It measures how much of a piece is repetition, and how exact. Copying raises it.
  7. Harmony forgets octaves, bass and melody. Same chords, different tune: still a stripe. Another key: no stripe, unless transpose is on.
  8. Tempo lines lie by factors. Read the picture. The estimate cannot see above 200 BPM.
  9. One number repeats by chance. Dynamics and loudness have one value per moment; their stripes and thumbnails mean less.
  10. It is all seen through a transcription and one piano. Wrong notes, pedal times and a single preset shape every view.
And then. When a view and your ear disagree, trust neither too quickly. Look at the raw aspect, listen again, and write down what you heard before you change a lever.
